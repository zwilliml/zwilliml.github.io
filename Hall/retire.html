MARKLEY RETIRES FROM MOUNT UNION AFTER 39 YEARS

For Immediate Release: 5-8-95

ALLIANCE, Ohio-- Last week, Mount Union College said good-bye to an outstanding professor who devoted 39 years of his life to teaching mathematics. Dr. William Markley, professor of mathematics at the College, was honored at a special retirement ceremony at 7 p.m. Saturday, May 6 in the Main Lounge of the Hoover-Price Campus Center.

While Markley's commitment to education was evident in the classroom, it didn't end there. Through the years, he participated in sabbatical leaves which took him to England where he studied math concepts, including partial differential equations, variational methods and integral equations.

In addition, he was one of the College's first recipients of a STAR grant which allowed him to study the math departments of several highly-recognized colleges and universities. ``I was able to visit Colgat University, Dartmouth College, Colby College and Bates College -- and I gleaned a great deal of information from their departments--things that helped our math department at Mount Union,''' he said.

Chad Bielawski, a junior math major at Mount Union, said, ``Dr. Markley commands a great deal of respect. His commitment to serving students is impressive, and he will be missed.'' Markley, who won the College's Great Teacher Award in 1981, joined Mount Union's faculty in 1956, earned his B.S. degree from Bucknell University in 1949, his M. Litt. degree from the University of Pittsburgh in 1956 and his Ph.D. degree from the same institution in 1968.

According to Markley, he'll miss ``the challenges of teaching and the great people at Mount Union'' most. Upon retirement, he hopes to travel south and to Pennsylvania visiting relatives. ``I also enjoy playing softball, ping pong, soccer and racquetball, so I hope to be doing a lot more of that as well,'' he said.
