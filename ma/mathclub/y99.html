Duane Farnsworth Presents Tomsich Research - Wednesday, April 12, 2000

The last meeting of the year was Wednesday, April 12th at 4:00 P.M. in room 100 of Tolerton and Hood. This meeting primarily consisted of a talk by Duane Farnsworth, in which he formally presented the results of the summer project Dr. Brugh and he worked on last summer with the aid of the Tomsich Grant. The talk was accessible to everybody for the most part. In fact, a lot of the talk focused on how mathematics research works, what it is like, etc. The material was interesting and anyone who wants to learn more about the Tomsich grant or undergraduate research in mathematics in general is especially encouraged to contact a faculty member.

Graduate School Talk - Wednesday, November 10, 1999

If you have ever wondered what it would be like to go to graduate school, then come to the next math club meeting. This coming Wednesday, November 10, at 6:30 P.M. in 107 Simpson, Miracle Mallet (formerly Miracle Conrad), who graduated in the spring of last year, will be giving a talk about graduate school. First, Miracle will talk about her experiences as a graduate student at Case Western Reserve University to date. This, will be followed by a question and answer session,and then there will be some time to socialize.

Here is an excellent chance to get an inside look at what graduate school is really like--find out about things that are not adressed in brochures (like how often graduate professors are available or direct comparisons between our undergraduate classes here at MUC and graduate level classes).

We are anticipating that the question and answer session will be particularly informative, so we encourage all those who are coming to think of some good questions.

Even if you are not interested in going to graduate school for mathematics, attending this meeting may still be well worth your time. For instance, if you are teaching high school you will most likely have to do some graduate work in order to maintain your certification. Also, I am sure that much of what Miracle will have to say is directly applicable to all graduate study (especially in the sciences).
