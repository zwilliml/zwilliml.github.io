The following announcement is from the June 14, 2011, edition of The Canton Repository:

Priests reassigned in Stark County parishes

Posted Jun 14, 2011 at 12:01 AM

The Catholic Diocese of Youngstown has made several new pastoral assignments for Stark County parishes.

Several priests will take on new assignments later this summer as part of the Catholic Diocese of Youngstown Parish Reconfiguration Plan.

Seven priests have been appointed by Bishop George V. Murry as pastor or associate pastor in Stark County parishes that will or already have merged, will collaborate, or parishes that remain as single parishes.

The Stark County parishes affected include:

- St. Peter, Canton: The Rev. Edward Beneleit, pastor, effective Aug. 1; the Rev. John Sheridan, associate pastor, effective July 1.

- Regina Coeli, Alliance and St. Joseph, Washington Township (Maximo): The Rev. Howard Ziemba, shared pastor, effective Aug. 1.

- St. Joseph, Alliance and St. Ann, Sebring: The Rev. Maciej Mankowski, shared pastor, effective Aug. 1.

- Sacred Heart, Harrisburg: The Rev. Nicholas Mancini, pastor, effective Aug. 1.

- St. Therese, Brewster, St. Clement, Navarre: Will merge as Holy Family Parish, effective July 1; the Rev. Michael Seifert, pastor, effective Aug. 1.

- St. Paul, North Canton: The Rev. Steven Ziegler, associate pastor, effective Aug. 1.

Sheridan was ordained in July 2010. He completed seminary studies at Pontifical North American College in Rome and recently returned from Rome after completing studies in Liturgical Theology at the Pontifical University of the Holy Cross.

Ziegler will be ordained to the priesthood June 25 at St. Columba Cathedral. St. Paul’s parish is his first priestly assignment.

Also, the Rev. Donald Feicht, former pastor of St. Benedict parish in Canton, and most recently at St. Joseph parish in Alliance, has been appointed administrator of St. Mary and Joseph parish in Newton Falls, effective Aug. 1.

Feicht also served as Dean of Stark County East Deanery and on the diocesan Priests Council and Priest Retirement Board.

The reconfiguration plan was introduced in May 2010 as a pre-emptive measure to address the decline in membership within the diocese, as well as the shrinking number of available priests.

Under the plan, the six-county diocese, which serves 216,151 Catholics, has reduced the number of its parishes by 25 percent.

"Collaborative parishes" means that churches will maintain their separate identities but share a single priest.

"Merged" parishes means that two or more parishes will combine their resources to create a new parish. The remainder will continue as single parishes.

Stark County has 22 parishes.
