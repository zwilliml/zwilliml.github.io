2005 St. Joe Softball Game Recap and Box Score

Rhinos Edge Trumbull Courier 12-11

Extra-Inning Nightcap Win Completes Doubleheader Sweep

St. Joe squeaked past Trumbull Courier 12-11 in 8 innings to win their fourth game in a row. The Rhinos scored 11 runs in the first 3 innings, but then their bats went cold as the Newspapermen chipped away and battled back to tie the game.
In the decisive 8th inning, Jared Svette hit a one-out single, then scampered all the way to third on an outfield throwing error. That left the Couriers with little choice but to intentionally walk the next 2 men, setting up a possible force at any base with Eric Rupert coming to the plate for the Shepherds. But Rupert came through by skying a fly ball to deep left, and Svette trotted home on the game-winning walk-off sac fly.

Nate Krol and Jim Wolfe were each perfect for the Rhinos, going 3-for-3. One of Krol's hits was a double, and he added the intentional BB.

An umpire decision in this game was protested by the Rhinos, but since they won the game the protest is moot. In the Rhino 5th inning with one out and men on second and third, Brad Dangerfield lofted a fly ball to the outfield which was caught. Both runners appeared to tag up and advance, with the runner on third scoring. But the Couriers appealed that Jeff Wilson left second base too soon, and the umpire agreed and called him out. But then the umpire erroneously ruled that the runner from third was not allowed to score, and this decision was promptly protested by Head Rhino Al Ratini.

Ratini checked with league head umpire Cal Cavalcante after the game, who agreed with Ratini that the run should have been allowed to score. So the Rhinos played the last three innings with an insurance policy, because if they had lost, then the game would have been replayed from the top of the sixth inning on with the score 12-8 in favor of the Rhinos. But since the Rhinos won the game, there was no need for the protest, and the only negative fallout from the wrong decision was that it deprived Brad Dangerfield of a sacrifice fly.
