1997 St. Joe Softball Game Recap and Box Score

Roofers Nail Hippos 18-0

Game Under Protest

D & K Roofers shut-out St. Joe 18-0, but a protested play at the very start of the game could wipe it all out.

Mike Zwilling led off the game with a single. Steve Stiles followed with a long drive to right field. Zwilling tagged up, thinking the ball might be caught, but it sailed over the Roofer's head, and the race to home was on with Stiles nipping at Zwilling's heels.

Zwilling stumbled and fell rounding third, causing Stiles to bump him from behind. The catcher tagged out Zwilling, but Stiles retreated to third safely. Or so he thought. The umpire called Stiles out for "making contact" with Zwilling, invoking the rule on one runner passing another. Head Hippo Al Ratini protested the game. The relevant rule, section 8E, says:

The runner is out when he physically passes a preceding runner before that runner has been put out.
If the protest is upheld, the entire game might be replayed, with Stiles on third with one out in the top of the first. Once before a Saint protest was upheld, but that time, instead of replaying the game from the protested point, the league commissioner said that St. Joe could make the playoffs with only 9 wins instead of the usual 10. That didn't help much that year and is not likely to help this year.

The Roofers struck early and often, with 5 runs in the first inning, 7 more in the second, and another 6 runs in the third. The feeble Hippo offense generated only 5 hits, 2 of them from Zwilling. Tom Hill contributed a double.

UPDATE: The protest has been denied. The league does not require a written statement from the umpire at the time of the protest, so the umpire simply changed his story at a later time and said that the one runner **had** passed the other. The league should institute a written report policy to avoid problems like these.
