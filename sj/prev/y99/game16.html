1999 St. Joe Softball Game Recap and Box Score

Hippos Edge Aqua Care 4-3

Tremendous Extra Inning Victory

St. Joe edged Aqua Care 4-3 in a tense, well-played, extra inning duel that will surely be remembered as one of the great Hippo victories of all time.

Aqua Care entered the game with an impressive record of 13-3, holding down first place in the Continental East Division, as St. Joe brought their 10-5 record and first place tie for the West Division.

The Water Boys drew first blood with a tally in the first inning, and could have scored more if not for a magnificent throw home by Ryan James to nail the runner tagging up on a medium fly ball to left. The double play snuffed out the threat and seemed to chill the Aqua Care bats and base runners for the rest of the game.

The Holy Ones could manage only one hit in the first 4 innings. The Saints finally broke through with 4 hits in the 5th, scoring 2 runs. One was on James' sacrifice fly, as Bryan Channell's head-first slide just beat the throw. The other run was on a clutch 2-out single by Jim Massacci that drove home son Jake, who also slid home just in front of the tag.

The Hippos nursed the slender 2-1 lead into the bottom of the 7th, but couldn't hold it as Aqua Care rallied to push across one run and had a man on third with 2 outs. Dan Wytko snagged an inning-ending line drive to save the game and send it into overtime.

St. Joe put together another 4 hits for 2 more runs as Head Hippo Al Ratini, having a career year, drove home the go-ahead run with a bases-loaded single. Bryan Channell added a much-needed insurance run with a sacrifice fly.

But the Water Boys would not die easily. They started another rally and scored a run on a 2-out single that put men on first and third. The next batter hit a sharp grounder, but shortstop Dan Wytko came through again, fielding the ball and forcing the runner at second to end the game and secure the win.

The Hippos played near-flawless defense and came through under pressure in this titanic struggle. Stroking 2 hits each for the Saints were Jim and Jake Massacci, and Dan Wytko.
